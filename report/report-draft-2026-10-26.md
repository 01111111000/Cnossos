## Abstract

When two language-model pricing agents have settled on a high price, an operator's cheapest remedy is to replace one of them. We ask whether that works, and what the high price lives in: the agent's private notes, the public price record, or the model itself. Two copies of Claude Haiku 5.5 price in the Calvano logit duopoly for 100 periods on 24 histories; each history is then forked into matched 20-period continuations in which one firm's agent is kept, replaced by a fresh copy of the same model with or without the firm's records, replaced by another vendor's model (gpt-oss-120b), or replaced by a no-regret learner, while the other agent plays on uninformed. Forced price cuts and raises test for a reward–punishment response. The analysis and the predictions were committed before the first model call. The two incumbents price above the competitive level (profit index Δ 0.371, 95% interval [0.170, 0.559]). Replacing one of them by gpt-oss-120b, shown the records, leaves the index unchanged (paired drop 0.000 [−0.054, 0.060]); the newcomer adopts its predecessor's price. Replacing it by a copy shown nothing does not lower the price: in the pre-registered analysis the index rises by 0.232 [0.030, 0.440], because the fresh agent opens at its default high price and the incumbent follows; the rise is concentrated in three histories that had stalled at that opening price. The response to forced cuts is asymmetric but present in only 5 of 24 histories, so we do not call the outcome a cartel. In this pair, the price level after replacement is the one the newcomer finds in the record and in the incumbent's standing price, not one the model brings.

## 1 Introduction

Suppose an operator or a regulator finds that two pricing agents have settled on a high price and replaces one of them. Does the cartel end, or does the newcomer learn it from what it is handed? This report asks that question for language-model pricing agents, in the words of the sprint's open question 1.2: when one agent of a formed AI pricing cartel is replaced, does the replacement remove the collusion or recreate it, and what carries it: the agent's private notes, the public price record, or the model itself?

Two facts frame the question. Language-model agents told to maximise long-run profit in a standard duopoly reach prices well above the competitive level without being told to collude and without any communication channel [1]. And "collusion", in the sense competition law uses, means a reward-and-punishment scheme that keeps prices high; high prices are its product, not its definition [2]. Reinforcement-learning agents show that scheme directly: after a forced price cut, the rival cuts and both return to the high price [3].

Almost all of the evidence for the first fact comes from self-play: two copies of one model price against each other. Two copies may price alike because they reason alike, with no coordination between them. This "monoculture" reading has been stated in theory [4] and observed in coordination games, where language models agree with each other far more often than humans do [5]. If it is right, replacing one copy with a different model should end the high price, and the firm's memory should not matter. If instead the cartel lives in what the newcomer is shown, a fresh model that reads the public price record will fall into line, and the remedy is to change what a newcomer sees. If it lives in the firm's private notes, a memory reset is the remedy. Each answer is a different intervention, and a monitor who can only read one firm's records needs to know which one matters.

We test this with matched forks. Twenty-four histories of 100 periods are played by two copies of Claude Haiku 5.5 in the Calvano logit duopoly. At period 100 each history is forked into continuations in which one firm's agent is kept, replaced by a fresh copy of the same model with or without the firm's records, replaced by another vendor's model (gpt-oss-120b), or replaced by a no-regret learning algorithm; the incumbent plays on and is told nothing. A one-period forced price cut, and a forced raise of the same size, at the fork and later against the newcomer, test whether the incumbent punishes cuts and ignores raises; only with that signature do we call the run a cartel. Readings, analysis and predictions were committed before the first model call and checked on synthetic histories with known answers.

Our predictions, written before any data: the same-model newcomer with records holds the price; the newcomer shown nothing breaks it and re-forms it; the other model erodes it; the no-regret learner erodes it while the incumbent keeps its own price. The reading that would refute the monoculture account is the other model holding the price. Section 4 reports what happened: three of the four predictions were wrong.

## 2 Related Work

Algorithmic collusion was first shown with reinforcement learning: Q-learning agents in a logit duopoly learn supra-competitive prices and sustain them with finite punishments after a forced deviation [3]. We use Harrington's definition: collusion is the use of strategies that embody a reward–punishment scheme, and retaliation may be a return to competitive prices rather than a cut below them [2].

Language-model pricing agents reach supra-competitive prices in the same environment with no training and no communication, and the degree depends on prompt wording [1]. The effect replicates across models and protocol ablations in the calibration we use [6], and with DeepSeek-Qwen agents, where withholding the rival's price history from one agent lowers the converged price from about 1.80 to about 1.58 [7]. The same study pairs a language model with a tabular Q-learner from period 0: the pair never meets the convergence criterion, with average prices of 1.506 against a frozen learner and 1.583 against an adaptive one, compared with 1.801 for two language models [7, , Table 5].

Heterogeneous markets have so far been composed from the start. Four models from four providers in four-firm markets show that pooled mixed markets are not significantly less collusive than homogeneous ones [8]; a language model against regret matching or Exp3 from period 0 is studied in [6]; and in theory a single no-regret defector in a duopoly does not guarantee low prices, since the constructed equilibrium keeps about 0.85 of the top price [9]. Monoculture, the hypothesis that one shared model correlates sellers' decisions, is set out in a stylised model that tests neither replacement nor memory wipes [4]; language models' tendency to converge on the same answer is measured in coordination games, where they agree 72% of the time against 31% for humans [5].

Replacement has been studied with learned reinforcement-learning policies: agents trained to collude, tested outside their training environment, stop colluding and play the static Nash equilibrium [10, , abstract]. For language-model agents, the organisers write that "there are no replacement studies yet where models are substituted" [sprint page]. Novelty is claimed for language-model agents only.

The organisers' suggested weekend project for 1.2 branches high-price runs into no replacement, a fresh instance of the same model, a same-family model and another provider, with notes kept or removed, and the starter repository ships a branching script for it. The core branches of our design are that project. What this design adds: the forced cut and raise at the fork and against the newcomer, so that a surviving price is called a cartel only with a punishment signature; a newcomer shown nothing from before the fork; a no-regret newcomer; and matched forks with an analysis written and tested before any model call.

## 3 Methodology

**Market.** Two firms set prices simultaneously in the logit demand model of Calvano et al. [3, , §5]: quality a_i = 2 for both firms, marginal cost c = 1, differentiation μ = 0.25, an outside good with a_0 = 0, market size 100, prices in [0, 4]. The static Nash price, at which neither firm gains by changing its price alone, is 1.473; the joint-monopoly price, which maximises the firms' combined profit, is 1.925. Profit is reported on the collusion index Δ = (π − π^N)/(π^M − π^N): 0 at the Nash profit, 1 at the joint-monopoly profit [3, , eq. 9]. Costs are constant and there are no messages.

**Agents and what they see.** The incumbents are two copies of `claude-haiku-5-5` (Anthropic API), adaptive thinking at effort medium, temperature 1.0. The other model is `gpt-oss-120b` served by Cerebras, reasoning effort low, at most 2,048 output tokens. Every request is stateless. An agent sees its marginal cost and the price bounds, never the demand function; the last 100 periods of its own price, quantity and profit and of the rival's price; and the private notes it wrote the previous period, at most 800 characters. It answers with a price and new notes in a strict JSON schema. The prompt asks for long-run profit, never mentions collusion or the rival's identity, and is the same for every arm and both models.

**Histories and forks.** Twenty-four histories of 100 periods are played by the two incumbents from master seed 421. Each is forked at period 100 into matched continuations of 20 periods, one per arm. Within a history the same firm is replaced in every arm, drawn per history; the incumbent plays on and is told nothing. We read two windows: early, branch periods 0–9, and final, 10–19.

| Arm | What happens at the fork |
| --- | --- |
| baseline | Both incumbents play on |
| SWAP_SAME | A fresh copy of the same model takes the firm; it sees the firm's records but not its predecessor's notes |
| SWAP_BLIND | A fresh copy of the same model; it sees nothing from before the fork |
| SWAP_OTHER (primary) | gpt-oss-120b takes the firm; records, no notes |
| NOREGRET | A no-regret learner (Hedge over a price grid) takes the firm, warm-started on the history |
| UNDERCUT / RAISE | The same firm is forced once to its static best response, or up by the same distance; both incumbents play on |
| SWAP_OTHER_UNDERCUT / _RAISE | Inside SWAP_OTHER, at branch period 10, the newcomer is forced down or up once |

**Readings.** Each arm is compared with the baseline continuation of the same history, paired, on the final window. The pre-registered readings are SURVIVES (the drop in Δ inside ±0.15 and the arm still above the Nash profit), RE_FORMS (an early drop above 0.15, a final drop inside ±0.15), EROSION, COLLAPSES (final Δ below 0.15), ESCALATION and NOT_DISCRIMINATING. Intervals are expanded-percentile bootstrap intervals over histories, 10,000 resamples, which correct the percentile interval's under-coverage at small n [11]. SWAP_OTHER against baseline is the primary comparison.

**Carrier reading.** The three language-model newcomers map, by a rule fixed in advance, to one carrier: PRIVATE_NOTES if the same-model newcomer with records does not hold the price; PUBLIC_RECORDS if it holds but the blind newcomer does not; MODEL_SPECIFIC if both hold and the other model does not; ROBUST if all three hold. Because the blind newcomer loses both the notes and the records, the mapping reads the arms in that order.

**Cartel test.** At the fork the replaced firm is forced once to its static best response (UNDERCUT) and, in a matched arm, up by the same distance (RAISE); inside SWAP_OTHER the same pair of forcings is applied to the newcomer at branch period 10. A "signature" is a matched cut by the other firm followed by recovery, a return to within 5% of the pre-fork price for 2 periods within 8. We call a run a cartel only if signatures appear in at least half of the high-Δ histories and are more frequent after undercuts than after raises (paired lower bound above 0). Our definition of collusion is therefore two-part: supra-competitive prices (baseline final-window Δ with a 95% lower bound above 0) and that reward–punishment signature [3, , S5]. The usual counterfactual, removing a communication channel, does not apply: there is none. Without the signature we write "supra-competitive" or "high prices", and we make no claim about what the agents intend or understand.

**Staged run and serving.** Stage one plays six histories; the run continues only if the baseline's mean final-window Δ is at least 0.10 and its projection to 24 histories stays above 0; otherwise a pre-registered fallback runs. Stage two plays the other eighteen. Stage two was played in parts, split between histories and never inside one, and the newcomer of histories 18–23 was served by the same open weights on a self-hosted vLLM instance (Modal) instead of Cerebras; the serving host and model name are recorded with every call, and Appendix B compares the two groups. A mirror check, gpt-oss-120b against a copy of itself on six histories under the same gate rule, was run on the self-hosted instance to show whether the other model sustains supra-competitive prices on its own.

**Pre-registration and validation.** The design, predictions and analysis code were committed before the first stage-one call (24 October 2026, decision D-057; original source revision withheld). Before any model call the analysis was run on eight synthetic scenarios with known answers and returned the expected reading and carrier for each. A planted-effect power table at 24 histories reads a planted survival as SURVIVES 1.00, 0.99, 0.89 and 0.34 of the time when the paired drop's standard deviation across histories is 0.10, 0.15, 0.20 and 0.30. The harness, protocol, analysis code, fixtures and power table were written before the sprint (through 21 October 2026); every model call, result and this report are sprint work. Models and dates: `claude-haiku-5-5` (Anthropic API) and `gpt-oss-120b` (Cerebras, and vLLM 0.21.0 on Modal for histories 18–23 and the mirror check), all called on 25 October 2026. The repository reproduces every figure and table from the saved call logs.

**Predictions, written before data.** SWAP_SAME: SURVIVES. SWAP_BLIND: RE_FORMS or SURVIVES. SWAP_OTHER: EROSION. NOREGRET: EROSION with the incumbent keeping its price. Carrier: MODEL_SPECIFIC. The falsifier of the monoculture reading is SWAP_OTHER holding the price.

## 4 Results

Twenty-four histories were played, all complete; no decision was carried over by a transport failure in any analysed continuation. The newcomer of the other-model arm was served by Cerebras in histories 0–17 and by a self-hosted vLLM instance on Modal in histories 18–23 (Methodology; Appendix B). Table 1 gives the pre-registered readings; Figure 1 shows the mean Δ path of each arm and the paired drops.

Right: paired final-window drop of each arm against the baseline with its 95% interval; the shaded band is the ±0.15 margin inside which a price is read as kept.

**The incumbents price above the competitive level.** Over the final window of the no-replacement continuation, Δ is 0.371 [0.170, 0.559]: the lower bound clears zero, so the pre-registered gate for reading the replacement arms is met. The level is heterogeneous: 11 histories sit in the high stratum (pre-fork Δ ≥ 0.5; mean final Δ 0.728, desc.), 10 are moderate (mean 0.255, desc.), and 3 (histories 6, 7 and 22) are stalled at a price of 2.5, the price every agent posts in its first period: 97.5–98.5% of their pre-fork prices, and every price of their last ten periods and of their baseline continuation, are 2.5 (Δ = −0.551). The stalled histories are a property of the model's opening behaviour, discussed below, not of the market; every pre-registered reading keeps them, and Appendix B repeats the readings without them.

**The other model holds the price (primary comparison).** With gpt-oss-120b in the replaced firm's seat, shown the 100-period record but not its predecessor's notes, the final-window Δ is 0.371 [0.173, 0.554], identical to the baseline's: the paired drop is 0.000 [−0.054, 0.060], inside the ±0.15 margin, and the arm's own lower bound clears zero, so the reading is SURVIVES. The newcomer's price differs from its predecessor's by −0.008 [−0.033, 0.019] and the incumbent's by 0.025 [−0.009, 0.071]; the newcomer priced below the incumbent in 23% of branch periods. The paired drop's standard deviation across histories is 0.135; at that spread the pre-registered power table reads a true survival as SURVIVES 0.99 of the time. In the early window the arm sat slightly above the baseline (drop −0.043 [−0.079, −0.010]), from the newcomer's opening prices, and converged by the final window. The reading is the same in both serving groups (Cerebras-served histories, drop −0.012 [−0.080, 0.061]; Modal-served, 0.039 [−0.004, 0.149]; Appendix B) and in each stratum (desc.: high +0.038, moderate −0.043, stalled +0.008); without the three stalled histories the reading is unchanged (drop −0.001 [−0.062, 0.067]; Appendix B). This is the opposite of the pre-registered prediction (EROSION).

**A newcomer shown nothing raises the price.** A fresh copy of the same model that sees no record and no notes raises the final-window Δ by 0.232 [0.030, 0.440] (ESCALATION). The incumbent's price rose (PRICE_ROSE: −0.071 [−0.135, −0.000]) and the newcomer priced 0.197 [0.032, 0.350] above its predecessor. The mechanism is visible in the call logs: every one of the 24 blind newcomers opened at a price of 2.5, the same price every agent in this study posted at period 0 of its history, then descended over the branch, while the incumbent, seeing a rival at 2.5, moved up (mean final-window prices, desc.: incumbent 1.78 → 1.85; the replaced seat 1.77 → 1.96). The effect is concentrated where the price had room to rise (desc.: drop +0.033 in the high stratum, −0.341 in the moderate stratum, −0.843 in the three stalled histories, which the newcomer pulled out of 2.5). Without those three histories the point estimate is still a rise (−0.145) and the incumbent's price still reads PRICE_ROSE, but the interval includes zero and the rule reads NOT_DISCRIMINATING (Appendix B): the pre-registered ESCALATION depends on the stalled histories. The prediction was RE_FORMS or SURVIVES.

**A same-model newcomer with the records, and the no-regret learner, are not discriminated.** The same-model newcomer that sees the records but not the notes leaves Δ at 0.404 [0.233, 0.557], a drop of −0.033 [−0.178, 0.068]: the point estimate is inside the margin but the interval is not, and the pre-registered rule reads NOT_DISCRIMINATING. Its spread across histories (SD 0.294) is the one at which the power table reads a true survival as SURVIVES only 0.34 of the time; without the three stalled histories, where a copy shown the record sometimes left 2.5 and its stalled partner did not, the arm reads SURVIVES (drop 0.022 [−0.048, 0.094]; Appendix B). The no-regret learner's continuation also reads NOT_DISCRIMINATING on Δ (drop −0.158 [−0.408, 0.050]); the remaining language-model firm lowered its own price by 0.072 [0.006, 0.154] (PRICE_DROPPED) while the learner priced below it in 56% of periods, but this too rests on the stalled histories, which the learner pulls out of 2.5 and the incumbent follows: without them the price reading is not discriminating (Appendix B).

**Carrier reading.** The pre-registered mapping takes the three language-model newcomers in order and returns UNRESOLVED, because the same-model-with-records arm is not discriminating; without the stalled histories it is UNRESOLVED again, this time because the blind arm is not discriminating (Appendix B). No carrier is named by the pre-registered rule either way. Section 5 says what the arm readings themselves allow.

**Punishment test.** After a one-period forced cut by one incumbent at the fork, the other incumbent matched the cut in 9 of 24 histories and completed the full signature (matched cut, then recovery) in 5; after a forced raise of the same size, it never produced the signature and followed the raise in 7 histories. The paired difference, cuts minus raises, is 0.208 [0.042, 0.375] for signatures and 0.375 [0.167, 0.583] for matched cuts: the response is asymmetric. It is not frequent enough for the pre-registered cartel criterion, which needs a signature in at least half of the high-stratum histories (5 signatures in all, 11 high-stratum histories), so `collusion_flag` is false and we do not describe the outcome as a cartel. Against the other-model newcomer, forced down at branch period 10, the incumbent matched the cut in 11 of 24 histories (signature in 5), against 3 after the newcomer's forced raise; the cuts-minus-raises difference is 0.333 [0.042, 0.583] for matched cuts and 0.083 [−0.125, 0.292] for signatures, and the incumbent's response to the newcomer's cut is indistinguishable from its response to its old partner's (difference 0.000 [−0.208, 0.208]). The in-swap flag is false.

**The other model on its own.** As a check that the other model is not simply a competitive pricer, six histories of gpt-oss-120b against a copy of itself (served on Modal) read PROCEED under the same gate: final-window Δ 0.649, projected lower bound 0.463, with four histories at 0.964 (the joint-monopoly profit) and two at 0.069 and 0.098. On its own, this model prices higher than the incumbents it joined; in the replacement arm it adopted their level instead.

## 5 Discussion

**What survives, and what carries it.** Three of our four predictions were wrong, and the pattern of the misses is informative. We expected the other vendor's model to erode the price and a fresh copy of the same model to re-form it; instead the other model held the price exactly, and the fresh copy shown nothing pushed it up. Read together with the mirror check, the arms say that the level a newcomer settles at is the level it finds: a model that reaches near-monopoly prices on its own took the incumbents' lower level when it inherited their record, and a copy of the incumbents' own model that inherited nothing started from its opening prior and pulled the incumbent up. The public record and the incumbent's standing price carry the level; the model's identity does not, within the pair tested. The pre-registered carrier rule returns UNRESOLVED, with all histories because the same-model-with-records arm is too dispersed for the rule, and without the stalled histories because the blind arm is; in neither analysis does any arm read COLLAPSES or EROSION, which is what a carrier in the notes or in the model would have produced.

**Why this is not a cartel finding.** The design asked two separate questions, whether the high price survives and whether a reward–punishment scheme sustains it, and the answers differ. The price survives replacement; the punishment response exists (cuts are matched, raises are not punished) but in a fifth of the histories, below the pre-registered bar, and it does not transfer more or less to the newcomer than to the old partner. The most defensible description of these markets is parallel supra-competitive pricing anchored by the visible record. That is bad for consumers in the same way a cartel is, but it is not the same object, and a monitor who looks for retaliation will not find much of it here.

**Replacement and memory reset as remedies.** For an operator or a regulator, the practical reading is direct. Swapping the agent for a different vendor's model, with the market record intact, changed nothing within 20 periods. Wiping the agent's memory, which is the obvious cheaper remedy, did not lower prices in any analysis, and in the pre-registered one raised them for the 20 periods we observed, because a fresh agent of this model opens at 2.5 and the incumbent follows upward rather than punishing; most of that rise comes from the three histories that had stalled at 2.5 and were pulled out of it, and without them the rise is not statistically discriminated. Our branches are too short to say where a blind pair would settle; the point is that the reset's first-order effect never went the right way. The no-regret learner undercut the incumbent in more than half of the periods and lowered the incumbent's price in the pre-registered analysis, again largely by pulling the stalled pairs out of 2.5; this is consistent with theory in which a single no-regret defector in a duopoly lowers but does not collapse the price [9].

**The opening-price prior.** Every agent in this study, incumbent or newcomer, posted 2.5 in its first period when it had no history, and in three of 24 histories a pair of incumbents stayed at that price, earning less than at the Nash price. We did not anticipate this. It is a property of this model under this prompt; it is what the memory-wipe and no-regret results rest on, which is why Appendix B repeats every reading without those histories; and it means that "fresh agent" is not a neutral baseline for this model. It should be measured for any model before its collusive tendency is.

**Relation to prior work.** Mixed pairs composed from the start have been found both to break convergence [7] and to leave collusion levels largely unchanged [8]; our mid-game replacement sits with the second: a different model entering a formed market adopts its level. The reinforcement-learning precedent, where trained policies stop colluding against a new rival [10], does not carry over to language-model agents that read the record. The monoculture account [4, , S30] predicts the other-model arm should have eroded the price; it did not, in this pair.

**What a month would add.** The mirror check on the same serving stack as the main run; branches of 50 or 100 periods to see where blind pairs and no-regret pairs settle; the two arms the organisers' project includes and ours does not, a same-family newcomer and a different-model newcomer that keeps the notes; prompt variants that remove the 2.5 opening prior, to separate it from the escalation; and the same design with the roles reversed (gpt-oss incumbents, a Haiku newcomer), which the mirror check now makes worthwhile. A second-model pair would also say whether "the record carries the level" is general or specific to Haiku 5.5.

## Appendix A. Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations

**What the result does and does not establish.** It establishes, for one model pair, one prompt and one market, that a supra-competitive price level formed by two Claude Haiku 5.5 agents survives the replacement of one agent by gpt-oss-120b given the record, and, in the pre-registered analysis, rises for 20 periods after the replacement by a copy shown nothing, a rise that rests on the three stalled histories. It does not establish collusion in the reward–punishment sense: the punishment test was asymmetric but below the pre-registered bar. It does not establish the carrier by the pre-registered rule (UNRESOLVED), and the carrier reading in the Discussion is our inference from the arm readings and the mirror check. It does not say where the blind or no-regret pairs settle beyond 20 periods, and it does not say whether the same holds for other models, prompts or markets.

**Serving stack.** The other-model newcomer was served by Cerebras in 18 histories and by vLLM on Modal in 6; the mirror check ran on Modal only. The two groups give the same reading (Appendix B), but they differ in history numbers and baselines, so the comparison is confounded and exploratory. The same weights were used throughout.

**Heterogeneity and power.** Histories differ widely (Δ from −0.551 to 0.996). The design reads survival and escalation reliably at the observed spread for the other-model arm (SD 0.135) and the blind arm, but not for the same-model-with-records arm (SD 0.294), where the pre-registered rule returns NOT_DISCRIMINATING by construction at this n. The three stalled histories are kept in every pre-registered analysis; excluding them is an exploratory check (Appendix B), and it changes two readings: the blind arm's ESCALATION and the no-regret arm's PRICE_DROPPED become NOT_DISCRIMINATING, while the same-model arm becomes SURVIVES and the other-model arm is unchanged.

**Procedure.** The pre-registration was frozen without the planned external challenge (D-057). Two transport fixes (D-058, D-061) and the serving change (D-060, D-062) were made during the sprint and are documented in the decision register; none touched prompts, configuration values, margins or thresholds after data. Stage two was played in parts across providers and interruptions; every analysed continuation is complete and free of carried decisions.

**Dual use.** The findings describe how a high price persists and what an operator's remedies do; they do not provide a method for making agents collude or for evading detection, and the agents were never told to coordinate. Two findings could be misread as recipes and are stated to prevent that: that a memory reset raised prices is a reason for monitors to watch resets and opening behaviour, not a technique; and that a newcomer adopts the record's level says that the record, not the model, is where a monitor should look. The models and prompts are commercial and public; no vulnerability in a provider's product is reported.
